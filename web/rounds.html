Design Principles

The system does the work

Data gathering is automated. The only manual input is the one thing AI can't do: the bedside exam.

Yesterday vs today

Every patient gets a plan-vs-reality check. What did we say? Did it happen? What carries forward?

Discharge-oriented thinking

Every day plan starts with the discharge blocker. The question is always: what gets this patient closer to home?

Acuity-appropriate

ICU patients get detailed alerts and contingencies. Routine post-ops get discharge checklists. The system adapts.

Why I Built This

I'm a physician who left clinical practice to work at the intersection of medicine and AI. Not to advise from the sidelines — to build.

This agent exists because I've been the resident at 5 AM, coffee in hand, opening twenty charts before rounds and trying to figure out what changed overnight, what's critical, and what the plan is for each patient — all before the attending shows up.

The Consult Agent handles the acute question: "Is this patient sick?" Rounds Prep handles the daily question: "What changed? What's the plan?" Together they cover the two core workflows that consume a surgery resident's cognitive bandwidth.

I built this with Python, the Anthropic API, and FHIR R4 — no templates, no drag-and-drop. Every clinical decision, every alert threshold, every plan item reflects real surgical training.
