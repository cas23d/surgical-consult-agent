Design Principles

Eyes and ears, not autopilot

The agent surfaces what the resident might miss at 2 AM — it doesn't make decisions. The clinician stays in the loop.

Evidence should be traceable

The workflow is designed to surface the basis for recommendations. Any citation or clinical recommendation still requires clinician verification.

Built for messy reality

Residents type in shorthand and chart data is incomplete. The design makes uncertainty and missing information visible instead of hiding it.

Readable prototype

Plain Python and JavaScript keep the workflow inspectable. The repository shows the prompts, FHIR adapter, model call, and interface directly.

Why I Built This

I'm a practicing physician who builds at the intersection of clinical care and AI. I use AI-assisted development to turn workflow problems into prototypes that clinicians and product teams can inspect.

This agent exists because I've been the resident at 2 AM staring at a consult page, pulling together labs, imaging, and vitals from six different screens while trying to think clearly about a sick patient. That experience informed the workflow and the human-review checkpoints.

The Consult Agent focuses on the acute question: "How sick is this patient, what information is missing, and what requires immediate clinician attention?"

I directed AI coding agents through product definition, implementation, debugging, and deployment while owning the clinical workflow, prompt design, and evaluation criteria.

I'm interested in teams where clinical depth, product judgment, and rapid AI-assisted prototyping all matter.
