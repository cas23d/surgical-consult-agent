# Video Walkthrough Guide

Create a 2-3 minute demo video showcasing your Surgical Consult Agent. This video is one of the highest-impact pieces for impressing health tech hiring managers.

## What to Record

A screen recording showing:
1. The consult demo loading a case
2. Editing exam findings in the textarea
3. The AI agent streaming outputs in real-time
4. Navigating between triage, context, plan, and final note stages
5. How the design is optimized for "hour 28 of a shift"

## Script (2-3 minutes)

Time it out beforehand so you stay on pace.

---

**[0:00-0:10] Open and Introduction**

*Show the live demo site loading*

"This is the Surgical Consult Agent — an AI tool I built for surgery residents to triage new consults at 3 AM when cognitive reserve is gone.

Most clinical AI fails exactly when it's needed most. This one is designed for the worst moment, not the best."

---

**[0:10-0:30] Load a Case**

*Click on Case 1 (Perforated Diverticulitis)*

"I'll load a case. Here's the patient chart pulling data from the EHR — vitals, labs, imaging, medications — all automatically organized.

The key insight: at 3 AM, you don't have the bandwidth to navigate six different screens. Everything you need is here."

---

**[0:30-0:50] Show Triage Output Streaming**

*Show the triage stage completing and output streaming*

"The agent performs rapid triage: Is this patient sick or not sick? It identifies red flags — in this case, free air on CT, sepsis criteria, hemodynamic instability.

This isn't generic summary. This is what a senior surgeon would ask first."

---

**[0:50-1:15] Edit Exam Findings**

*Click on the resident input textarea and type custom findings*

"Now here's where it gets powerful. I can add my bedside exam findings — what I actually found on the patient.

[Type: 'Acute distress. Rebound and guarding across abdomen. On vasopressors since arrival.']

The system will use this to refine its assessment. Not replacing the resident. Augmenting what they already know."

---

**[1:15-1:40] Show Context and Plan Stages**

*Auto-advance to context stage, then plan stage*

"Notice each stage streams in real-time. It's not a 5-second wait for a response — you see the reasoning building, and you can start reading immediately.

The context stage surfaces gaps: What's been done? What's missing? What do we still need to know?

Then the plan stage — evidence-based recommendations with specific guideline citations. Not vague. Not hallucinated. Grounded in real clinical guidelines."

---

**[1:40-2:05] Show Final Note**

*Navigate to final note stage, toggle between summary and full view*

"The final output is a complete, copy-paste-ready consult note and staffing summary.

I've designed this with a principle: don't make the resident reformat anything. If the AI output requires editing before use, the system is incomplete.

This note goes straight into the EHR."

---

**[2:05-2:25] Closing: Design Philosophy**

*Pan to the "Design Principles" section on the homepage*

"Every system I built was guided by one constraint: it had to work at hour 28 of a shift, when cognitive reserve is gone and the margin for error is zero.

That's not a nice-to-have. That's the entire specification.

The breakthrough wasn't a clever prompt. It was understanding the actual moment of use."

---

**[2:25-2:35] Call to Action**

*Show contact info on screen*

"I'm looking for a role where clinical depth and engineering ability both matter. If that's your team, let's talk."

*Show: Email, LinkedIn, GitHub links*

---

## Recording Tips

### Tools
- **Mac**: QuickTime Player (built-in), Loom (cloud, shareable)
- **Windows**: OBS Studio (free), Camtasia, ScreenFlow
- **All platforms**: Loom is the fastest for sharing

### Setup
1. Close notifications and extra tabs
2. Set screen resolution to 1920x1080 (YouTube standard)
3. Zoom to 110-125% for readability
4. Use a dark theme (matches your site aesthetic)
5. Have the site loaded and ready before recording

### Recording
- Speak clearly and naturally (no robotic tone)
- Go slowly. It's okay to pause.
- Use mouse to highlight where you're pointing
- Record multiple takes — pick the best one
- Avoid background noise

### Pacing
- Each section should feel deliberate, not rushed
- Pause for 1-2 seconds before starting to speak
- Let outputs finish streaming before moving to the next stage
- Build narrative tension: problem → solution → evidence

## Post-Production

### Editing (Optional but Recommended)
1. Trim dead space at the beginning/end
2. Add subtle background music (royalty-free, low volume)
3. Include text overlays for key points
4. Add your name/title at the end
5. Keep total runtime 2-3 minutes

### Export Settings
- Format: MP4 (most compatible)
- Resolution: 1920x1080 (or native if higher)
- Bitrate: 5-10 Mbps (balances quality vs file size)
- Frame rate: 30fps

## Sharing

### Embed on Website
Add to your portfolio site's README or a dedicated "Demo" section:

```html
<video width="100%" height="auto" controls>
  <source src="consult-agent-demo.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  Your browser does not support the video tag.
</video>
```

### Upload to YouTube (Private/Unlisted)
1. Create a YouTube account
2. Upload video as "Unlisted" (only people with the link can see)
3. Copy the embed code
4. Add to your portfolio site

### LinkedIn
- Post a 15-second teaser
- Link to full video
- Caption: "Built an AI surgical consult agent designed for the worst moment (hour 28 of a shift). Here's how it works..."

### Email to Recruiters
- Keep it in your email signature or cover letter
- Frame it: "Here's a 2-min demo of a clinical AI system I built"

## What NOT to Do

- Don't add cheesy music or overly dramatic effects
- Don't use a monotone robot voice for narration
- Don't make the video too long (attention span is short)
- Don't use technical jargon without explanation
- Don't show errors or issues (record until it's perfect)

## Checklist

Before sending to anyone:

- [ ] Audio is clear (no background noise, good volume levels)
- [ ] Video is in focus and easy to read
- [ ] Total runtime is 2-3 minutes
- [ ] Pacing feels natural (not too fast, not too slow)
- [ ] All three demo stages play smoothly
- [ ] Closing with contact info is clear
- [ ] File format is MP4 or web-friendly
- [ ] Tested on phone, tablet, and desktop browsers

## After Recording

Send the video to:
1. **Hiring managers at target companies** — Include in your outreach email
2. **Your contacts in clinical AI** — Ask for feedback before the official version
3. **LinkedIn** — Post as a carousel of clips or full video
4. **Your portfolio site** — Embed prominently

The video is your secret weapon. Most candidates have a resume and maybe a GitHub repo. You'll have a **working demo** with you explaining the clinical reasoning behind every design decision.

That's the difference between "looks interesting" and "I need to talk to this person."
